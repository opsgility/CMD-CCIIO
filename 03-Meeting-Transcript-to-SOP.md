# Demo + Try: Convert Meeting Transcript to SOP (UC4)

**What you'll do:** Generate a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) or job aid from a meeting transcript or discussion.

**Time:** 8-12 minutes

**Outcome:** Reduce SOP drafting time by 60-80%.

---

## Setup

1. Open **Word** (or **OneNote** if you prefer) on your work machine
2. Click the **Copilot icon** on the Word ribbon (right side)
3. Have a meeting transcript or discussion notes ready (use a real Teams meeting transcript or the sample below)

---

## The Prompt (Copy & Paste)

```
Convert this meeting transcript into a step-by-step SOP for [your process name].

Format the SOP as:
- Title
- Purpose (1-2 sentences)
- Prerequisites/Roles involved
- Steps (numbered, clear, action-oriented)
- Decision points (if/then guidance)
- Common issues & workarounds
- Who to contact for questions

Use professional, clear language. Assume the reader is new to this process.

Here is the transcript:

[PASTE TRANSCRIPT HERE]
```

## Sample Meeting Transcript

**Teams Meeting: Contract Modifications Review Process**  
**Date:** June 3, 2026  
**Attendees:** Tyrone (Contracts), Sarah (COR Lead), Miguel (Finance), Hung (Compliance)

---

**Tyrone:** OK team, let's document our process for contract modifications. We've been doing this ad-hoc and it's creating confusion. What's the current flow?

**Sarah:** When a contractor submits a modification request, it usually comes to me as the COR. I do a quick sanity check—is it in scope? Does it align with the PWS? Then I send it to Miguel in Finance for cost analysis.

**Miguel:** Right. Once I get the mod request and cost impact, I check our budget. If it's under $10K and within approved budget, I flag it as "ready for review." If it's over $10K or affects other budget lines, it goes to Tyrone for approval first.

**Tyrone:** And that's key—anything over $10K needs my signature before it moves to Compliance. Below $10K, Sarah and I can handle it together. Hung, where does Compliance fit in?

**Hung:** I see every mod request, but priority depends on type. If it's a pricing change only, it's quick—maybe 2 business days. If it touches scope, requirements, or delivery timeline, I need more time—could be 5 business days. I also check against our insurance and regulatory constraints.

**Sarah:** So the flow is: COR sanity check → Finance review and budget check → If >$10K, Tyrone approval → Compliance review based on complexity → Final recommendation back to me and the contractor.

**Miguel:** One thing—we should set a timeline expectation. How long should a contractor wait?

**Tyrone:** Standard modifications should be done in 2 weeks. Complex ones might take 3 weeks. We should communicate that upfront.

**Hung:** Agreed. And we should document which types are "simple" vs. "complex" so the COR can set expectations right away.

**Sarah:** Got it. So we need an intake form too—something contractors fill out so we get consistent information upfront.

**Tyrone:** Exactly. Let's use the template we built last year. Miguel, can you send that to everyone?

**Miguel:** Yes, I'll send it after this call.

**Tyrone:** Great. So to summarize: COR intake → Finance budget → >$10K decision gate → Compliance review (with timeline guidance) → Recommendation back to COR → COR notifies contractor. Clear?

**All:** Yes.

**Hung:** One more thing—escalations. What if there's disagreement? Like, Compliance says no but Finance says the cost is justified?

**Tyrone:** Good point. Any disagreement goes to me and we hash it out. I'll get back to the COR with a decision within 2 business days.

**Sarah:** Perfect. I'll draft a full SOP and we can review next week.

## What to Try Next (Follow-up Prompts)

After Copilot generates the SOP, try these refinements:

- *"Add a decision flowchart or table showing which path based on dollar amount and scope impact."*
- *"Create a RACI matrix showing who is responsible, accountable, consulted, informed."*
- *"Add estimated timeline for each step (e.g., 'COR review: 1 business day')."*
- *"What are the most common rejection reasons? Add a troubleshooting section."*
- *"Create a 1-page quick-reference card from this SOP."*
- *"Draft an email template the COR can use to notify the contractor of the timeline and process."*

---

## Tips

- **Capture key decisions:** Highlight who decides what and at what thresholds (e.g., >$10K).
- **Make it actionable:** Use imperative verbs ("Submit," "Review," "Approve") not passive descriptions.
- **Test for clarity:** Would someone completely new to this process be able to follow it?
- **Add context:** Include role titles, timeline expectations, and escalation paths.
- **Iterate with stakeholders:** Share the draft SOP with the people in the transcript to validate.
